# ADEEVA: Is There Real Scope? A Decision Brief for Shan

_Prepared 13 Oct 2026. Every figure comes from the fact-checked research. Unverifiable claims were dropped and corrected figures are used. **[Uncertain]** marks a weak or partial point._

---

## 1. Bottom line

There is real scope, but it is narrow, and trust is the whole proposition. Parents are measurably worried about strangers, harassment, spending pressure and data. ADEEVA's offline design with no data, no ads and no chat answers those worries, and it also keeps it outside almost every child online-safety law. Demand for a fashion-craft game has just been proven: Dressmaker launched on 21 Sep 2026 and is estimated at about 372K copies. That also makes Dressmaker a strong direct competitor. The weak link is money. No source shows parents of teens paying extra for "safe" games, and most "safe studio" successes are preschool apps. The realistic path is a small, low-cost business: a premium one-off purchase with a free demo, offline community features and real-world sewing tie-ins. It would grow into a small portfolio on one engine. Do not expect a quick income.

---

## 2. The parents who worry

**Who they are**

- **US parents of children 12 and under.** About 8 in 10 say social media's harms outweigh its benefits. 67% say tech companies should do more to set rules for what kids see and do online, and 55% say lawmakers should (Pew, n=3,054, May 2025).
- **US parents of under-18s.** 85% say their kids have access to games. 48% say games are mostly positive for their kids and 15% mostly negative. 77% co-play at least monthly, 66% set daily time limits and 65% restrict game types (Marist, n=876, June 2025). **They are selective about games, not against them.**
- **Teen girls, ADEEVA's actual players.** About three-quarters of US teen girls play games: 81% at ages 13-14, falling to 67% at 15-17. Only 17% call themselves "gamers" and only 22% play daily (Pew, fieldwork Sept-Oct 2023). They are an underserved group.

**What they fear**

| Fear                          | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strangers and contact         | 60% fear harmful adults could contact their child through games (PlaySafe ID, 2025). **[Weak]** The study comes from a verification vendor and published no sample size. 59% of UK parents worried about strangers (Ofcom 2022, now dated).                                                                          |
| Harassment                    | 43% of teen players have been harassed and 80% of teens call it a problem (Pew 2024).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Screen time and sleep         | 41% of teen players say games have hurt their sleep (Pew 2024). In Marist, screen time is the top concern for 15% of parents.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Spending and loot boxes       | 51% of UK parents worried about pressure to buy in-game (Ofcom 2022), and 43% about loot boxes (Internet Matters, 2021). **Counterpoint:** only 5% of US parents name cost or in-app purchases as their _top_ concern (Marist 2025).                                                                                 |
| AI companions                 | 72% of US teens have tried one (Common Sense Media, 2025). Common Sense says no one under 18 should use them.                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Appearance pressure for girls | Dove (brand-commissioned, correlation only): 80% of girls had used a filter by age 13. 48% of girls who regularly distort their photos have low body esteem, against 28% of those who don't. In 2017 the Nuffield Council raised alarm about makeover and cosmetic-surgery "games" targeting girls as young as nine. |

Regulators and courts act on these fears:

- The FTC's Epic settlements totalled $520M: $275M for children's privacy (COPPA) and $245M for dark-pattern purchases.
- HoYoverse settled for $20M (proposed Jan 2025) over loot boxes and children's data.
- At least four US states sued Roblox in 2025. Tennessee's suit survived a motion to dismiss on 22 Sep 2026.
- About 177 federal cases are consolidated in Roblox MDL 3166 (a law-firm count, Sept 2026).

**How they choose:** 78% of US parents whose children play regularly use ESRB ratings (ESA 2025, an industry source). 81% of parents game with their kids (ESA 2026).

**What they will pay for [Weak evidence]**

- Kids' studios built on trust have made "no sneaky stuff" subscriptions work. Pok Pok reports six-figure monthly recurring revenue and raised about $9M (TechCrunch 2024). These are **preschool** products.
- 66% of US parents said they would subscribe to a platform with suitable content (SuperAwesome, 2021-22). That survey was about **video streaming**, not games, and the US/UK sample covered ages 4-15.
- 76% of European parents say their children make _no_ in-game purchases (Ipsos / Video Games Europe 2024). Parents are the buyers, and an up-front purchase they approve fits that.
- **The gap:** no survey or sales data shows teen-game parents paying a premium for "safe." Treat that as an untested assumption.

**Long shots, not to plan around:**

- **Apple Arcade:** invites unreleased games through a request form. Payment terms are not published. Keep a future title unreleased if you want to pitch it.
- **Google Play Pass:** invite only.
- **Netflix:** removed about 34 indie games in 2025 and its kids app is for ages 8 and under.
- **Grants (UK Games Fund £100-250K, Creative Europe, Canada Media Fund):** all need a registered company, and the UK fund needs PAYE staff and matched funding. They are not realistic yet.

**Avoid:**

- ads and ad SDKs
- in-game currency
- loot boxes and gacha clothing packs (PEGI 16+, gambling issues in Belgium, the HoYoverse settlement)
- limited-time offers (PEGI 12)
- login streaks that punish missed days (PEGI 12)
- urging children to "buy now" (ASA rulings against Moshi Monsters and Bin Weevils)
- "For Kids" wording in app-store metadata outside the Kids Category (Apple guideline 2.3.8)

Toca Boca World shows that free-to-play with in-app purchases can work even for a family brand, since its spending recovered in 2025. It is volatile, though (-16.8% in Q3 2024), and it contradicts the trust promise.

## 4. Community: a safe design ladder

| Step | Feature                                                                                                                                                                               | Legal and moderation cost                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0    | **Parents-only community**: newsletter, devlog, an adults-only Discord or group                                                                                                       | Low. Keep children out, because off-platform contact is where risk moves (New Jersey sued Discord in 2025).                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 1    | **Offline exports**: a lookbook PNG, a runway card, a printable pattern that the family shares themselves                                                                             | About zero. No data leaves the game.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| 2    | **Local family play**: hot-seat "family runway", a parent as judge or client                                                                                                          | Zero. Fits how families already co-play.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 3    | **Design codes** (like Animal Crossing design IDs) built only from structured data (pattern, fabric, colours, preset decorations) and decoded on the device. No free text, no images. | Near zero while there is no server. Sharing happens outside the game.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 4    | **Curated showcase**: entries submitted to Shan, who publishes the ones Shan selects                                                                                                  | Provider-published content is outside the UK Online Safety Act. Collecting submissions _is_ processing personal data: you need a privacy policy, consent and a retention policy. Under GDPR Article 8, a 15-year-old needs parental consent in Germany, Ireland and the Netherlands. COPPA applies if under-13s are likely. |
| 5    | **Closed groups on a server** (class or club codes, Minecraft Education style)                                                                                                        | Becomes a user-to-user service: UK Online Safety Act access and risk assessments (reviewed yearly), reporting and blocking, UK Children's Code privacy defaults, the Apple 1.2 user-generated-content rules and Google Families social-feature rules.                                                                       |
| 6    | **Public gallery with preset reactions only** (hearts, "runway ready")                                                                                                                | Everything in step 5, plus moderation work. Automated screening is cheap (AWS about $0.001 per image) but not sufficient. OpenAI's image moderation does not cover sexual content involving minors.                                                                                                                         |
| 7    | **Free chat or image uploads**                                                                                                                                                        | **Do not build this.** The baseline is now Roblox-style facial age checks. Known-abuse image detection (Thorn Safer Match) costs $30,720 a year. The litigation exposure is shown above.                                                                                                                                    |

Recommendation: build steps 0-3 now. Step 4 is optional later, after a short privacy-lawyer review. Treat 5 and above as a separate business decision. One more consideration: girls are less likely than boys to say games help their friendships (35% against 55%), so steps 1-3 matter for keeping girls playing.

## 5. Regulation checklist

**Applies now (offline, no data, sold as a product)**

- [ ] **Keep "no data" literally true.** Self-host fonts and assets: Google Fonts and third-party CDNs send IP addresses. No analytics, no crash reporters that send device IDs.
- [ ] **Store paperwork:** a privacy policy, Apple's "Data Not Collected" privacy label, and Google's Data safety form and target-audience declaration.
- [ ] **Age rating:** free through IARC for Google Play and other digital stores. Apple uses its own questionnaire. Aim for a low rating.
- [ ] **PEGI interactive-risk criteria (from June 2026):** no paid random items, no time-limited offers, no streak penalties.
- [ ] **Marketing claims:** describe the activities, not outcomes (FTC Jungle Rangers order, UK ASA/CAP rules). Do not use "For Kids" metadata outside Apple's Kids Category. The Kids Category covers ages 11 and under, so ADEEVA would not belong in it.
- [ ] **Safety claims must match the product exactly.** The Roblox and Discord suits are consumer-protection cases about safety claims.
- [ ] **If on US app stores with in-app purchases:** handle the age and consent signals required by the App Store Accountability Acts, rate each purchase, and notify the stores when monetisation changes. A web or itch.io release without in-app purchases avoids most of this.
- [ ] **[Not researched]** VAT and sales tax on direct web sales; consumer law on digital content sold to minors. Check before selling outside Steam, Apple and Google.

**Applies if online features are added (accounts, cloud saves, analytics, gallery, chat)**

- [ ] **Amended COPPA rule**, enforceable from 22 Apr 2026, if under-13s are likely users: verifiable parental consent, separate consent before any third-party sharing, written retention and security policies.
- [ ] **UK Children's Code:** applies to under-18s, so a 15-year-old counts. High-privacy defaults, no nudges, a data protection impact assessment.
- [ ] **GDPR Article 8:** parental consent for consent-based processing under 13-16, depending on the country.
- [ ] **UK Online Safety Act:** children's access and risk assessments (codes in force since 25 Jul 2025), moderation, reporting and blocking.
- [ ] **EU Digital Services Act Article 28:** a micro-enterprise is likely exempt **[one legal reading disagrees]**.
- [ ] **Apple 1.2 and Google Families social-feature rules:** filtering, reporting, blocking, an in-app safety reminder and adult action before sharing.
- [ ] **Watch list:** COPPA 2.0 (passed the Senate, would extend to teens; status after June 2026 unverified); California's Age-Appropriate Design Code (partly revived in March 2026); the EU Digital Fairness Act (proposal expected Q4 2026); the EU consumer-protection network's principles on virtual currencies.
- [ ] **Get a short privacy-lawyer review before launching any of these.**

## 6. Benefit: what can honestly be claimed

**Safe to say:**

- "You sketch, choose fabric, measure, cut, sew, price, pay rent and run a label."
- "Practise budgeting decisions."
- "No ads, no chat, no data, works offline."
- "Designed for creativity and skill, not looks. Diverse bodies, no appearance scoring." Only say this if it is true in the game.

**Do not say** "teaches financial literacy," "improves maths," or "builds entrepreneurs." The evidence:

- Games beat non-game teaching by a modest margin (meta-analysis, g = 0.33, k = 57, K-16 studies from 2000-2012), and results depend on design.
- Studies of simulations teaching finance are small or involve university students. None shows it works for teens.
- Most apps sold as "educational" score low on the Four Pillars of Learning (active, engaged, meaningful, socially interactive). Designing against those pillars is a defensible way to stand apart.

**Real-world tie-ins**

- **Sewing and repair revival:** 59% of Gen Z can do basic clothing repairs, against 79-81% of older generations (Levi's/Burke survey, n=601, a brand survey). Levi's free _Wear Longer_ curriculum (grades 9-12, launched Jan 2026) is a possible partner or model. Fortune (Feb 2026) reports Gen Z taking up mending to get off their phones. US secondhand apparel grew 19% in 2025 (ThredUp/GlobalData, an interested party), which suits an upcycling theme.
- **Libraries:** teen and adult makerspace sewing courses exist. Peters Township Library's course earns a sewing-machine certificate. **[Only one example was verified, so "many libraries" is not established.]** Pitch: "design it in ADEEVA, sew it at the library."
- **4-H Fashion Revue:** young people model garments they made themselves, judged on construction and poise. Rules vary by county. It is a natural "real runway" partner.
- **Girl Scouts:** the Senior Textile Artist badge is confirmed. Check the GSUSA Badge Explorer before pitching anything else.
- **Schools:** Family & Consumer Sciences, Design & Technology and textiles lesson packs.
- **Sewing kits and fabric brands:** **[No precedent found.]** This is open ground but unproven.

## 7. Portfolio concept: one engine, one safety promise

Each game uses the same loop: brief, design, materials, measure/cut, assemble, decorate, show/sell, run the business. All are offline, with no ads, chat, data or currency.

1. **ADEEVA (fashion):** the flagship career and label sim, with runway shows.
2. **Thrift-Flip Studio:** buy secondhand, unpick, upcycle and resell. Ties into the repair and resale trend and ADEEVA's fabric systems.
3. **Patisserie Atelier:** design cakes, measure ingredients, bake and decorate for client orders, then run a bakery. Real recipes are printable.
4. **Workshop & Wood:** furniture design, cutting lists, joinery and finishing, with measurement and geometry built in.
5. **Little Architect / Interior Studio:** client briefs, floor plans, materials budgets and a show-home reveal.
6. **Jewellery Bench:** sketch, choose materials, set stones, price, and build a collection for a market stall.

Later, once there are 3 or more titles, sell them as a family bundle. Hold back one unreleased title as a possible Apple Arcade pitch.

## 8. A 90-day plan (small, local-first)

**Days 1-30: prove it at home**

- Watch your daughter play in 3-4 sessions without helping. Note where she gets stuck, what delights her, and whether she would show a friend. Retune the quality bars from what you see.
- Audit the "no data" claim: self-host fonts, confirm zero network requests, and add a short "What this game does NOT do" page.
- Add step-1 sharing: a lookbook PNG export and one printable "sew it for real" pattern.
- Check the body-image positioning: diverse body types and no appearance scoring.
- Run a short local name and trademark check on "ADEEVA" **[not researched]**.

**Days 31-60: small outside test**

- Release a free web demo on itch.io: the first career chapter. No analytics, so collect feedback through an opt-in form for **parents** or an email address.
- Have 5-10 families you know test it, ideally with teen girls aged 13-17. Ask parents whether they would pay $10-15 and what would make them recommend it.
- Write a one-page pitch for a local library makerspace, 4-H club or sewing school ("design in game, sew it for real") and run one session.
- Start an adult-facing devlog or newsletter (community step 0).

**Days 61-90: decide whether to commit**

- If testers return unprompted and parents say they would pay, prototype the Electron build and create a Steam page (the $100 fee is recoupable). Start collecting wishlists. Plan a Steam Next Fest demo several months out.
- Build design codes (step 3) only if testers ask to share.
- Sketch the shared engine for title #2 (Thrift-Flip is the cheapest because it reuses fabrics), but do not start building it.
- Re-check Dressmaker's ports and features and sharpen ADEEVA's angle: career and label story, runway, teen and family safety, browser and tablet play.

**Decisions only you can make**

- **Money:** the price ($10-15 or other); whether to stay hobby-scale or invest in marketing; whether you ever accept in-app purchases (recommended: fixed-price expansions only).
- **Legal:** whether and when to incorporate, which affects grants, tax and liability; whether any step 4+ online feature is worth a lawyer review and the ongoing duties; where to sell first (web/itch avoids app-store age-law duties).
- **Brand:** the umbrella studio name and the exact wording of the safety promise, which you must keep forever; whether to market to parents, teens or both (15+ teens pick their own games, so the game must appeal to her, not only to you); how public your daughter's involvement is (recommend keeping it private).

**Main risks:** Dressmaker's head start (est. ~372K copies, 98% positive, ports planned); discoverability (cozy-tagged Steam releases were up 65% in 2025); the unproven willingness of parents of teens to pay; and drifting into online features.
