## Research line (after Interior and Chef; reference and study only)

1. **Reference research:** 2–3 top-rated titles per game genre with their itch.io and Steam ratings, review counts and official screenshot sources. This feeds Phase C.
2. **Competition and go-to-market study** for itch.io and Steam: direct competitors per game (price, ratings, sales signals, tags), how comparable family-friendly cozy games launched (demos, Next Fest, wishlists, pricing, bundles), and store rules (age ratings, fees, requirements).

The line only produces one report for Shan. Nothing is created or published: no store pages, accounts or listings. Next steps are discussed only if Shan is confident in the output.

## Phase B — Depth (promised to Shan; do not skip)

Each slice game grows to ADEEVA's depth:

- **Second career or show mode** as a playable progression, the way ADEEVA has Student → Label:
  - Thrift-Flip: market stall → resale boutique
  - Patisserie: bakery → patisserie competition and tea salon
  - Workshop: commissions → furniture showroom and fair
  - Jewellery: stall → collection launch
  - Interior: room makeovers → show-home development
- **More content:** clients, materials, products, upgrades and story chapters, about twice the slice content.
- **Progression links:** reputation thresholds that unlock the second career inside the same save.
- **Economy and difficulty:** retuned with a simulated player, aiming for a 75–90% pass rate for a decent player on Standard.

## Phase C — Quality loop (two agents at a time)

For each game, one game at a time or two in parallel:

1. **External references:** a separate agent fetches official screenshots from Steam store pages and studio press kits. For each game it picks 2–3 named, highly rated titles in that game's genre and records each image's URL and fetch date. The images stay in the session scratchpad, are used for internal comparison only, and are never copied, traced or committed.
2. **Matched pairs:** each of our screenshots is paired with a reference showing the same kind of screen (hub with hub, crafting step with crafting step, results with results) at the same size.
3. **Blind judging:** a script randomly assigns each pair the labels A and B and strips filenames and logos. A fresh critic agent that has never seen our code picks the better image on fixed criteria (art, typography, feedback and juice, readability for ages 8–15+, touch comfort), explains each verdict and names concrete defects. The labels are revealed only after judging.
4. **Pass bar:** a game passes when our screen wins or ties at least 60% of pairs and no pair is judged "clearly worse".
5. **Improver loop:** otherwise an improver fixes the named defects, then all the usual checks run. New screenshots, new random labels and a fresh critic follow, for up to 4 rounds. Any gap left after that is reported plainly.
6. **Final external judge:** real kids playing the game. Their reactions outrank any agent's verdict.

Out of scope: 3D or ThreeJS rewrites and AAA-shooter comparisons. These games are 2D SVG cozy sims by design.
